\section*{Technical Abstract}

\noindent Project title: Directional Reflectance Profile Analysis for
Laid-Line Detection in Historical Paper\\
Author: Zhanfeng Zhou\\
College: TODO\\
Supervisor: TODO\\[1em]

Handmade European paper carries, embedded in its structure, a
fingerprint of the mould on which it was formed. The closely
spaced laid wires and the more widely spaced chain wires of the
mould produce a small but reproducible periodic thickness
modulation on the dried sheet, with a density and a wire diameter
that vary from one mould pair to another and can therefore be
used by codicologists to date, localise and group otherwise
undated manuscripts. The standard workflow for measuring these
features is manual counting under raking light, which is slow,
observer-dependent, and exposes fragile material to repeated
handling. Multispectral image (MSI) archives and directional
reflectance microscopy (DRM) acquisitions now exist at scale at
major holding institutions including Cambridge University
Library, but no robust automated pipeline turns these into the
quantitative mould-fingerprint data that codicology actually
requires.

This project closes that gap with a single detection pipeline
that supports two distinct kinds of input. The multi-phi DRP
variant is the original target and operates on the full
directional reflectance stack produced by the AddME Lab DRM
instrument in the Department of Engineering: it aggregates
normalised power spectra across all available azimuthal views,
recovers a single dominant period from the aggregated spectrum,
fits the per-view phase with a polarity-aware correction, and
combines the per-view phases into a weighted circular mean with a
half-period auto-correction step. The single-image MSI variant is
the extension that lets the same pipeline run on the legacy
single-frame archives held by libraries: it applies a radial
fast-Fourier transform followed by a single Gabor cleanup pass to
the same effect. The two variants share a common preprocessing
and evaluation framework: background subtraction, automatic
line-direction detection by spectral-power scan, a polarity flag
and a configurable period search band; together with an
evaluation bundle that reports for every folio the harmonic-fit
$R^2$, the empirical interval distribution, a self-contrast
$z$-score against rotated null directions, the per-segment wire
full-width-at-half-maximum, and for multi-phi input additionally
a split-half period stability across random partitions of the
azimuthal views.

Detector accuracy is first characterised on a synthetic phantom
that places periodic Gaussian wire shadows at a controlled
period, orientation, wire width and additive noise level. Period
recovery is within $\pm 2.5\,\%$ across the full range of
laid densities encountered in practice (10 to 50 pixels per
period), essentially invariant to noise down to a signal-to-noise
ratio of two, and accompanied by a single failure point at the
top of the period search band that is identified later as the
sub-harmonic alias mode. Orientation recovery is within one
degree across the full range of laid-line angles, and wire-width
recovery is within ten per cent for wire sigmas between two and
five pixels.

The pipeline is then applied to two real-folio benchmarks. The
first is a set of nine MSI folios drawn from four manuscript
stocks in the Cambridge University Library collection,
catalogued by the Manuscripts Lab in the Faculty of English under
the Mapping Paper in Medieval England project. The catalogue
spreadsheet records one density per stock; the pipeline
disagrees with the spreadsheet by between ten and seventy per
cent on seven of those folios. On the two further folios for
which an independent per-folio manual count was carried out, the
pipeline agrees with the manual count to within one per cent.
The seven larger errors are therefore attributed to within-stock
mould-pair variation that the catalogue spreadsheet does not
resolve, rather than to detector failure. The second benchmark
is a group of four DRP datasets at higher magnification:
dataset 10 is a sheet of paper hand-made by Professor Da Rold
using a reproduction of a fifteenth-century European mould, and
the pipeline reports 8.31 lines per centimetre on it with a
strong metric bundle (self-contrast $z = +4.05$); the remaining
three datasets are zoomed-in real folios that illustrate the
crop and period-range recipes that recover trustworthy density
estimates from low-signal acquisitions.

The agreement between the pipeline and the manual count on the
two calibrated folios is well within the manual count's own
precision of about $\pm 1$ wire per centimetre, which is the
threshold at which mould-pair identification by density alone
becomes possible without recourse to the chain-line spacing or
watermark geometry that the classical codicological workflow
relies on. The metric bundle accompanying
every density estimate makes the confidence in the estimate
inspectable rather than tacit, so that low-signal folios can be
flagged automatically rather than silently mis-reported.
Identified directions for further work include automatic
detection of the sub-harmonic alias mode, automatic detection of
the polarity flag, joint chain-line analysis to expose a richer
mould fingerprint, and confidence-aware aggregation across the
folios of a complete codex.
