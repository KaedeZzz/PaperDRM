\subsection{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:conclusion:findings}

The project set out to recover a quantitative mould fingerprint of
laid-line density, wire diameter and local orientation from a DRP
acquisition of a historical paper folio, with no per-folio
parameter tuning and with a quantified confidence for each
estimate. The detection chapter of \Cref{sec:multiphi} delivers
this through two related variants: the multi-phi DRP variant that
was the original project target, and the single-image MSI variant
that lets the same pipeline run on the single-frame archives
already held by major institutions. The headline figure is that
the pipeline reproduces a per-folio manual count of every MSI
folio in the benchmark to within ten per cent on eight of nine
folios, closer than the catalogue spreadsheet manages on six of
the same nine folios. The ninth folio, Ff.4.15 f24r, is a
sub-harmonic alias failure that the metric bundle flags
unambiguously. The agreement between the pipeline and the
manual count sits inside the roughly twelve per cent
inter-counter variability between the spreadsheet and the new
manual count, which is the inherent uncertainty of hand-counting
laid wires on real manuscript paper. The synthetic-phantom
characterisation of \Cref{sec:benchmark:msi:phantom} establishes that
the period estimator is essentially noise-invariant down to a
signal-to-noise ratio of two, accurate to within $\pm 2.5\,\%$
across the period range of every paper density encountered in
practice, and accurate to within one degree in orientation. The
controlled reproduction sample of \Cref{sec:benchmark:drp:zoomed}
confirms that these accuracies hold on real paper made under
known mould specifications. The discrepancies of up to seventy
per cent observed against the stock-level spreadsheet catalogue
are explained, on the evidence of the nine-folio manual count,
by the intrinsic difficulty of laid-line hand-counting on this
material (two independent expert hand-counts of the same folio
disagree by a median twelve per cent) together with a contribution
from within-stock mould-pair variation rather than by detector
failure.

\subsection{Reflection on the project}
\label{sec:conclusion:reflection}

Two design decisions, taken before the experimental work began,
made the rest of the project tractable. The first was building
the pipeline as a sequence of self-contained stages with
inspectable intermediate outputs. Adding a new detector at
\Cref{sec:multiphi} did not require touching the loader or the
evaluation, and replacing the original projection autocorrelation
in the line-direction detector with the spectral-power criterion
was a single-file change. The second was building the synthetic
phantom framework early. The harmonic-doubling bias of the legacy
Gabor pipeline of \Cref{sec:first_attempts:legacy} was visible on
a phantom run in a single afternoon and would have been very hard
to identify on real folios alone.

The most significant thing that did not work as planned was the
ground-truth strategy. The project began with the assumption that
the Mapping Paper spreadsheet would be a per-folio reference, and
the gap between that assumption and the reality of stock-level
recording was discovered only once seven of nine benchmark folios
returned spreadsheet disagreements in the same direction. The two
manual-count folios that resolved the gap were added late in the
project. With hindsight the project would have organised manual
ground truth collection in the Lent term so that the entire
real-folio benchmark could have been read against per-folio
references from the start.

The other reflection concerns the relationship between the
pipeline and the metric bundle. The five evaluation metrics of
\Cref{sec:multiphi:evaluation} were originally designed as
diagnostics for the developer, to help debug the detector during
development. Over the course of the project they grew into a
deployment-grade output: the bundle is what allows a downstream
user to read whether a density estimate should be trusted, and the
self-contrast $z$-score is what flagged the alias on dataset 2 and
the low-signal regime on Ff.4.9 f42r. Treating the metric bundle
as a primary output rather than an internal tool, from the
beginning of a future project of this kind, would shape the
detector design more than the present sequence did.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:conclusion:future}

Four extensions are immediate. The first is the automatic
sub-harmonic alias detection identified as the chief limitation in
\Cref{sec:discussion:limitations}, which would remove the need to
narrow the period range by hand on folios such as Ff.4.15 f24r and
make the no-tuning ambition of \Cref{sec:bg:contributions} strict
rather than approximate. The second is an automatic polarity
detector that would set the \texttt{wire\_is\_darker} flag from
the broadband signal statistics, removing the last item of
per-modality user input. The third is joint chain-line detection,
which the present pipeline does not attempt; chain lines together
with laid lines would give a richer mould fingerprint and would
support direct mould-pair matching rather than density-only
identification.

The largest single extension, and the natural sequel to the
present project, is a confidence-aware aggregation across the
folios of a complete codex. Given a per-folio image of every
folio of a manuscript -- whether a single MSI frame or a full
DRP stack -- the pipeline already returns a density and a metric
bundle per folio; combining these into a per-codex profile would
expose mould changes mid-codex, support binding-order
reconstruction, and surface the kind of stock-level variation that
this project has been able to demonstrate only indirectly. The
infrastructure for that aggregation is straightforward; what is
needed is the calibrated manual ground truth across a larger
sample of folios to validate the confidence calibration of the
metric bundle at scale. Once that calibration is in place, the
pipeline becomes deployable as a service that a holding institution
could run over its existing MSI archive or a new DRP acquisition
without specialist intervention.
