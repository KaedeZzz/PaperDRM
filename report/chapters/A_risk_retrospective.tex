The risk assessment submitted to the Safety Office at the start of
the Michaelmas term identified the project as a computational
study without any physical experimentation. The principal hazards
recorded were the standard ones for laptop-based work: prolonged
use of a display screen, posture and repetitive-strain risks
associated with long typing sessions, and the wellbeing risks
associated with working alone for extended periods. No hazards
related to physical specimens were recorded, because the project
was set up from the outset to use pre-existing imagery shared by
the AddME Lab and the Manuscripts Lab; the report's author did not
need to handle, transport, or scan any manuscript material
directly.

The hazards actually encountered during the project tracked the
assessment closely on the physical side. The display-screen and
posture risks materialised as expected during long development
and writing sessions, and were mitigated by the standard breaks
and a height-adjustable working setup. There was no contact with
manuscript materials, so the absence-of-physical-hazard prediction
held. One hazard category was, however, materially under-weighted
in the original form: project-management and cognitive-load risk.
Several development episodes -- in particular the debugging of
the DRP cache invalidation logic in Michaelmas, the
projection-autocorrelation bug in Lent, and the late discovery
of the spreadsheet-versus-folio ground-truth mismatch in Easter
-- led to longer continuous sessions than the original
risk-assessment timetable allowed for. The sleep and concentration
risk that this created is, in hindsight, a real category that
deserves its own line on the form rather than being folded into
"working alone for extended periods".

A second item that the original form did not anticipate is
software and data-management risk. The pipeline is meaningful only
to the extent that its output is reproducible, and a corrupted
DRP cache or an accidentally overwritten archive of results would
have a real impact on the project's deliverables. The project
mitigated this in practice with version control on the source
code, a cache-invalidation serial that prevents stale outputs
from being silently reused, and per-folio result archives kept
under a deterministic directory structure. None of these
mitigations was named in the original assessment, and a future
version of the form would be better for including them
explicitly.

Two changes follow if the project were to be restarted. First,
the form should include a project-management hazard category
distinct from the personal hazard categories, covering scope
creep, milestone slippage, and the corresponding pressure on the
working schedule. Second, the form should include a
software-and-data hazard category covering data loss, cache
corruption and reproducibility, with the mitigations named
alongside.
